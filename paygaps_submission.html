Part A

Direction & Vision

Where I would take PayGaps visually, and the three concrete moves that would make it more intuitive without losing its credibility.

1. Look & Feel

PayGaps users are HR managers dealing with a topic that is new, legally significant, and unfamiliar. Many arrive already anxious: responsible for getting compliance right, uncertain where to start. The direction I am proposing, which I am calling Trusted Advisor, is designed to feel like a good accountant's office. Structured, unhurried, and credible. The kind of interface that signals you have done this before and so can they.

Practically: Plus Jakarta Sans for typography, clean and geometric without the clinical feel of Inter. A palette of PayGaps blue, cool neutrals, and a warm stone accent that removes the bureaucratic edge without losing trust. Color used sparingly, only for status, primary actions, and compliance data signals. Whitespace generous. Every screen carries a subtitle that tells the user exactly what they are here to do and why it matters.

The closest references are Personio (sober German HR professionalism) and Linear (complex workflows that always feel tractable). The goal is the combination of those two sensibilities.

3. Top 3 measures

1

A named compliance progress spine

User problemHR managers do not know what done looks like. Every screen feels like a new puzzle. There is no persistent sense of how far along they are or what comes next.
What it isA persistent vertical stepper in the sidebar showing the full compliance journey as named, sequenced steps with completion states and a step counter.
What it looks likeFive labelled steps below the main nav. Completed steps show a green checkmark. The active step is highlighted in PayGaps blue. Future steps are greyed with a lock icon. A small counter reads "3 von 5 Schritten." The user always knows where they are and what comes next without navigating away.
2

A plain language layer on every data point

User problemNon-technical users do not interpret numbers — they react to them. A Pay Gap of 3.2% means nothing to someone who does not know whether that is normal, alarming, or legally significant.
What it isEvery compliance metric is paired with a plain language interpretation, visible by default below the number — not hidden in a tooltip. It explains what the value means and whether it requires action.
What it looks likeA blue info callout applied consistently across all data screens. Below a Pay Gap of 3.2%, the callout reads: "Dieser Wert liegt unter dem gesetzlichen Schwellenwert von 5 %. Frauen in vergleichbaren Rollen verdienen durchschnittlich 3,2 % weniger." Repeated across sessions, it teaches users the concepts passively.
3

Purposeful microcopy on every screen

User problemUsers land on sections like Stellenkatalog or Gehaltsgruppen and immediately think: what am I supposed to do here? Generic labels do not communicate purpose, direction, or stakes.
What it isEvery screen gets a two-line header: the section name plus a subtitle that answers what you are doing here and why it matters for the compliance report. Empty states explain the next action.
What it looks likeUnder Stellenkatalog the subtitle reads: "Stellen definieren und bewerten — Grundlage für eine korrekte Pay-Gap-Analyse." Under Gehaltsgruppen: "Punktebänder freigeben — nur freigegebene Bänder gelten für die Analyse." Every empty table shows a clear explanation and a primary call to action. The language is calm, direct, and always action oriented.
Part B

Craft & Edge Cases

A full redesign of the Stellenübersicht across all three tabs, with proposed layouts, key states, and the reasoning behind each decision.

The three tabs form a sequence: define your jobs in Stellenkatalog, configure pay bands in Gehaltsgruppen, and map the organisational structure in Stellenarchitektur. The design challenge across all three is the same — make the information density legible to someone who is not a data analyst, and make the required action obvious without a manual.

Tab 1 — Stellenkatalog

Stellen definieren und bewerten — Grundlage für eine korrekte Pay-Gap-Analyse

3 ausstehend

The original Stellenkatalog puts too much work on the user before they can read a single row. Two separate color systems share no legend at table level — you have to scroll to the bottom of the page to understand what the colors mean. Draft rows are nearly indistinguishable from active ones. At eight columns, the table reads like a spreadsheet.

The redesign reduces cognitive load before the user reaches the data, and makes every row state immediately readable.

Key decisions

Legend moved above the table. In the original it sat below 20 rows, invisible until you scrolled. Moving it directly above means it is in view whenever the user is reading data. They learn the color system passively rather than retroactively.

GG badge colors unified. The original gave each grade a different hue with no semantic meaning. A single blue badge for standard grades and amber for custom grades (G16, G17) removes the false hierarchy.

Jobfamilie removed as a column. It now lives as a secondary line inside the Stellenbezeichnung cell. Same information, less table width, more room for the columns that matter.

Draft rows visually distinct. Stone background, muted text, italic "Noch nicht bewertet" in the score column, and a neutral badge. The user immediately knows which rows still need attention.

Bewertung simplified to three color stops. Red, amber, and green. The 61 to 75 vs. above 75 distinction does not change what the user does next, so collapsing it reduces noise without losing anything actionable.

Tab 2 — Gehaltsgruppen

Punktebänder freigeben — nur freigegebene Bänder gelten für die Analyse

2 freigegeben

The headline problem in the original is a single label: "15 auffällig." Fifteen out of seventeen bands flagged immediately reads as a crisis. But most of those fifteen are simply empty bands that have not been populated yet, which is entirely normal at this stage. The word "auffällig" does not distinguish between a real problem and expected behaviour.

The second issue is the status column, which shows two buttons side by side per row — Entwurf and Freigeben — with no signal of which is the current state and which is the available action. The redesign separates state from action across all rows, and replaces alarming language with accurate language.

Key decisions

"15 auffällig" replaced with "15 noch nicht freigegeben." Accurate, calm, and does not flag a problem that does not exist. The number stays the same. The interpretation changes completely.

State and action separated vertically in every row. The badge tells you what the row currently is. The button below tells you what you can do. They are visually distinct and never at the same prominence level.

G16 and G17 marked as Benutzerdefiniert inline. In the original they appeared at the bottom with red left borders, visually indistinguishable from errors. In the redesign they sit in their correct point-range positions with an amber label explaining their origin.

Empty grades de-emphasised, not hidden. GG1 to 3 and GG9 to 15 are shown with muted text and a secondary background. GG9 to 15 collapse into a single row. They are not removed, but they stop competing with populated grades for attention.

Band chart replaced with a status chip grid. One chip per grade, colour-coded by status, ordered by point range. Gives an immediate overview without requiring the user to decode a positional axis chart.

Tab 3 — Stellenarchitektur

Stellen nach Jobfamilie und Level einordnen — Grundlage für den Vergleich gleichwertiger Tätigkeiten (Art. 4 EU-RL)

3 Review offen

The matrix is conceptually the strongest tab in the product. Level cross Jobfamilie is immediately spatial and legible. Most users can orient themselves on first sight. The main issues are structural rather than conceptual.

Three jobs have no family assignment (Head of HR, Team Lead Sales, Senior HR Manager) and appear as plain Entwurf cards in the Ohne Jobfamilie column. The urgency of resolving them is not communicated at all. Empty rows at Director and Trainee level create visual uncertainty about whether the matrix is complete or broken. The review banner in the original reads like a collapsed settings panel rather than the most important action on the screen.

Key decisions

Three unclassified jobs surfaced as a named callout above the matrix. "3 Stellen ohne Jobfamilie" names the items, counts them, and provides a direct action. The issue is impossible to miss. Resolving it is one click per card.

Job cards simplified to two lines. Title plus headcount and track on one line with a status badge. The subfamilie subtitle was redundant given the column header and added noise in small cells.

Ohne Jobfamilie column header tinted amber. It reads as a temporary holding area, not a permanent column. A first-time user immediately understands that items here have not been placed yet rather than assuming it is a valid destination.

Matrix scrolls horizontally at a proper minimum width. Cards hold their proportions rather than squashing into the viewport. Readability is preserved at any screen size.
